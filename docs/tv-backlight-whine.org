#+title:     TV backlight coil whine (living-room Philips)
#+language:  en

* Overview

A high-pitched buzz / whine from the living-room Philips TV — a *TV hardware*
issue, fully independent of =krypton= / Kodi.  Documented because the diagnosis
is non-obvious and it surfaced during unrelated CEC debugging (see
[[file:kodi-cec-troubleshooting.org][kodi-cec-troubleshooting.org]]).

* Matching symptoms

- High-pitched buzz / whine from behind the panel, *below-center* (the
  power-supply / backlight-driver board), not the speakers.
- Independent of Kodi — muting Kodi (over JSON-RPC or otherwise) changes
  nothing.
- Independent of *TV* volume / mute, and of the TV-speakers-vs-external-audio
  setting.
- Present on *real* inputs (HDMI, *and* the tuner — even a black tuner screen)
  but *not* on the TV's internal smart-UI / apps.
- Cupping a hand over a tall transformer / inductor (black bobbin, yellow
  insulation tape) on that board damps it.
- Whine pitch / rate *tracks the backlight level*; gone at 100% backlight,
  degrades to a *clicking* at very low backlight.

* Root cause

*PWM backlight-dimming coil whine.*  Below 100% backlight the TV dims the LED
backlight by *pulsing* it (PWM); that pulsing current makes the
backlight-driver inductor mechanically resonate.  At *100% backlight* the drive
is continuous — no pulsing — so the inductor is silent.

A factory reset re-enables eco / auto-brightness defaults and a lower (PWM)
backlight, which turns a normally-faint whine loud.  It is not *new* after a
reset — just louder, and no longer masked by program audio.

* Fix

1. Set *Backlight = 100%* — the silent, no-PWM operating point.
2. If 100% is too bright, dim the *picture*, not the backlight: lower
   *Contrast* / *Brightness*, or use a *Movie/Cinema* picture mode.  These
   scale the video signal, not the LED PWM, so they darken the image while the
   backlight stays at silent full-duty.
3. Turn *OFF* everything that auto-dims the backlight, or PWM (and the whine)
   returns even with the slider at 100%:
   - Ambient light sensor / auto-brightness
   - Eco / energy-saving mode
   - Local dimming / contrast enhancement

* Identified component (for possible replacement)

The whine originates from a single magnetic component on the TV's power-supply
/ backlight-driver board, confirmed by damping it with a hand — the buzz stops
while the part is touched / covered.

Printed on part:

#+begin_quote
JSIS2.0 LTT3PCOXB070
F247881 BCK-33-194T
#+end_quote

Physical description:

- Tall through-hole component standing proud of the PCB.
- Black plastic bobbin / housing with a *yellow band*, and *yellow insulation
  tape* (Mylar / polyester) wrapped near the base toward the PCB.
- Located *below-center* on the board behind the panel.
- Function: the backlight LED-driver inductor / transformer.  Its current is
  PWM-modulated whenever the backlight is below 100%, which is what makes it
  sing (see [[Root cause]]).


Caveats before replacing it:

- This is a "working but mechanically resonant" part, *not* a failed one — the
  non-invasive fix (backlight 100% + auto-dimming off; see [[Fix]]) is
  preferred.  Swapping the magnetic is not guaranteed to silence it, and an
  exact replacement may resonate the same.
- Coil whine on a healthy magnetic is more commonly addressed by *staking* — a
  dab of non-conductive adhesive / varnish to mechanically damp the windings —
  than by replacement.

To source a replacement, capture the following (TV *unplugged and discharged* —
see [[Notes]] for the hazard):

1. The TV's *model number* (rear label).
2. The *board's* printed part number.  Philips / TPV power / backlight boards
   usually carry a =715G...= or =PLTV...= identifier, silkscreened or on a
   sticker.
3. The component's *reference designator* (silkscreen, e.g. =T1= / =L1= /
   =PT...=) and any markings printed on the part itself.

With the board part number and the designator, the board's service manual /
parts list maps it to an orderable component.

* Notes

- Do *not* use a backlight-*dimming* idle screensaver — dimming is exactly what
  causes the whine.  A plain black screensaver is fine (the backlight stays
  full).
- Working on the PSU / backlight board *live* is a shock hazard: the primary
  side and bulk capacitors carry lethal voltage and stay charged for minutes
  after unplugging.  Do all of the adjustment above from the on-screen menus,
  not inside the chassis.
